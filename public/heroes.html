Harper's Heroes

More than a name on a list.

Harper's Heroes is our promise to recognise children and young people who have faced childhood cancer, during treatment, after treatment and in remembrance.

What being a Hero means

We remember the child, not just the treatment.

The aim is not to keep bringing families back to cancer. It is to make sure the journey is acknowledged and that children have moments where the attention is on them for the right reasons.

A welcome packA proper welcome into Harper's Heroes.
Annual recognitionThoughtful contact around the awareness dates that matter to them.
Family experiencesInvitations to activities, events and opportunities as they grow.
Personal touchesGifts shaped around interests rather than generic assumptions.
A child can receive every part of Harper's Heroes without their story, photograph or diagnosis ever appearing publicly.
How it works

Simple for families. Careful behind the scenes.

1

Register or refer

A parent or carer can register directly. Someone else can make an initial referral so we can invite the family to complete their own details.

2

Family chooses

Families choose what they want to take part in, what information we can hold and whether anything can ever be shared publicly.

3

We keep showing up

Recognition dates, welcome packs, events and experiences are recorded so that support is planned rather than left to chance.

Remembering a Hero

Children who are no longer with us are still part of this.

Families can register a child who has died. We will handle that separately and sensitively, so parents are not asked to work through forms written as though their child is still in treatment.

Families will be able to choose whether they want annual remembrance, family invitations and ongoing contact. There is no expectation to share a story publicly.
